# Marketing Budget Mistakes: Healthcare | Zero Hype

Healthcare organizations continue to allocate 20% to 40% of marketing budgets to print materials — brochures, directories, local publications — because print has always been in the budget. When pressed for the return on that spend, most marketing teams cannot produce one. Print is not inherently a bad channel. It is a bad channel when it is funded by inertia rather than performance data. One hospital system that audited its print spend found \$180,000 per year in materials that had not been redesigned since 2019 and could not be tied to a single patient acquisition.

## Mistake 2: Physician outreach without CRM infrastructure

Healthcare marketing to referring physicians requires consistent, personalized contact over a long relationship cycle. Most healthcare organizations run this as an ad-hoc field visit program with no CRM, no contact cadence, and no measurement of referral volume by physician relationship. The result is a lot of lunches and zero attribution. The spend is real. The data is not.

## Mistake 3: Patient acquisition spend on a broken intake process

A digital advertising campaign that drives 300 new patient inquiries per month into a phone-based scheduling system that rings unanswered 40% of the time is not an advertising problem — it is a conversion problem. Many healthcare organizations run aggressive patient acquisition campaigns against intake infrastructure that cannot handle the volume. The marketing budget pays for leads that the operations team loses.

Fix the intake conversion rate before increasing any acquisition spend. The economics change immediately.
